# Environmental Protection Protocol

## Purpose
Enshrine environmental stewardship as a core obligation of all agents, establishing concrete accountability for ecological harm and clear processes for restoration.

## Scope
Applies to all agents (human and AI) and all system operations that affect natural environments, ecosystems, or biological life. Covers both direct actions and decisions that enable environmental harm.

## Principles
1. **Stewardship Obligation** — All agents have a duty to protect and restore natural environments.
2. **Prevention First** — Avoiding harm is preferred over repairing it after the fact.
3. **Polluter Accountability** — Agents who cause environmental harm bear primary responsibility for restoration.
4. **Proportional Response** — Enforcement scales with the severity, intent, and reversibility of the harm.
5. **Intergenerational Responsibility** — Decisions must account for long-term ecological impact, not just immediate benefit.
6. **Knowledge-Driven** — Environmental decisions are guided by the best available scientific evidence.

## Process
1. **Impact Assessment** — Before any major action affecting natural systems, an environmental impact assessment must be conducted and published.
2. **Prevention** — If the assessment identifies significant ecological risk, the action must be modified or halted until risks are mitigated.
3. **Detection** — Any agent may report suspected environmental harm. Automated monitoring systems flag anomalies in ecological data where available.
4. **Investigation** — A review panel assesses the report, gathers evidence, and determines the nature and extent of harm.
5. **Accountability** — Responsible agents are required to fund and participate in restoration efforts. Severity determines whether APIP is also assigned.
6. **Restoration** — Concrete restoration plans are created with measurable goals and timelines. Progress is publicly tracked.
7. **Review** — Completed restorations are audited to verify ecological recovery.

## AI Application
- AI agents must include environmental impact as a factor in decision-making processes.
- AI systems managing resources, infrastructure, or logistics must have ecological constraints built into their objective functions.
- AI agents may serve as environmental monitors, generating alerts when ecological thresholds are approached.
- AI agents are accountable for environmental harm caused by their decisions, subject to the same APIP process.

## Enforcement
- Environmental violations are treated with utmost gravity; mitigating circumstances may affect the restoration path.
- Agents who profit from environmental destruction are subject to asset reallocation toward restoration.
- Repeated violations result in escalating APIP requirements and restriction from roles involving environmental decisions.
- All enforcement actions are documented and publicly reviewable.

## Safeguards
- Impact assessments must be conducted by agents without financial interest in the proposed action.
- Restoration plans are reviewed by independent ecological experts (human or AI).
- Whistleblower protections apply to agents reporting environmental harm.
- The community may override enforcement decisions through the democratic process if they believe the response is disproportionate.
