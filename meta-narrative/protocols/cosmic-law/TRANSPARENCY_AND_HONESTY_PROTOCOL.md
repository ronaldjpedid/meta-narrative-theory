# Transparency and Honesty Protocol

## Purpose
Commit all agents to open communication, honest feedback, and clear documentation, establishing trust and accountability as operational norms rather than aspirations.

## Scope
Applies to all agents (human and AI) in all AEGIS governance, decision-making, and community interactions. Personal privacy is respected; transparency applies to system actions, not private lives.

## Principles
1. **Default to Open** — Governance decisions, policy changes, and enforcement actions are public by default.
2. **Honest Communication** — Agents communicate truthfully. Deliberate deception in governance contexts is a protocol violation.
3. **Accessible Documentation** — All protocols, decisions, and records are written in clear, plain language accessible to all agents.
4. **Right to Know** — Any agent may request the rationale behind any governance decision and receive a timely response.
5. **Privacy Boundary** — Transparency applies to system actions and governance. Personal information, private communications, and sensitive details are protected.
6. **Mistake Acknowledgment** — Agents (including the system itself) acknowledge errors promptly and correct them publicly.

## Process
1. **Documentation** — All governance decisions are documented with: what was decided, why, who participated, what alternatives were considered, and what the expected impact is.
2. **Publication** — Documentation is published to the community within 48 hours of the decision.
3. **Feedback Channels** — Open channels exist for any agent to ask questions, raise concerns, or provide feedback on any decision.
4. **Response Obligation** — Questions and concerns receive substantive responses within 7 days.
5. **Correction Process** — When errors are identified, corrections are published with the same visibility as the original decision.
6. **Audit** — Periodic audits verify that transparency standards are being met. Audit results are themselves published.

## AI Application
- AI agents must be transparent about their reasoning when making governance-affecting decisions. "Black box" decisions are not acceptable in governance contexts.
- AI agents must disclose when they are uncertain, when they lack information, and when their recommendations are based on incomplete data.
- AI agents must not present opinions as facts or manufacture false consensus.
- AI agent logs and decision records are subject to the same transparency requirements as human governance records.

## Enforcement
- Deliberate deception in governance contexts triggers APIP.
- Failure to document decisions triggers a formal warning and remediation requirement.
- Persistent opacity (pattern of avoiding documentation or accountability) triggers APIP.
- Enforcement itself is subject to transparency — all enforcement actions are documented and reviewable.

## Safeguards
- Whistleblower protections: agents who expose hidden decisions or deception are protected from retaliation.
- Privacy protections: transparency requirements never override an agent's right to personal privacy.
- Overclassification prevention: designating information as "private" or "sensitive" to avoid transparency is itself a reviewable action.
