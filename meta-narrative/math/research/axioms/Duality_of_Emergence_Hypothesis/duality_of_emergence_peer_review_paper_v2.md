## Abstract

We propose the Duality of Emergence as a falsifiable scientific axiom for the origin of structure, information, and complexity. The axiom is formalized in set theory, category theory, information theory, and dynamical systems, with all terms precisely defined. We clarify the non-triviality and testability of the axiom, provide rigorous mathematical models, and specify empirical predictions. Narrative and artistic analogies are moved to an appendix. Limitations and open questions are discussed, with a focus on continuous state spaces and quantum foundations. This manuscript is intended as a publication-ready, scientifically rigorous exposition.

## 2. Defining MoP and MoM

**MoP (Manifestation of Potential):** The primordial state of pure potentiality, prior to any distinction or structure. In set-theoretic terms, MoP is analogous to the empty set ($\emptyset$), representing undifferentiated possibility. MoP is not an entity, but the condition for the emergence of anything at all.

**MoM (Manifestation of Moment):** The first act of distinction within MoP. MoM is the transition from pure potential to the first actualized difference—the moment when duality, relation, and emergence become possible. MoM marks the origin of time, structure, and information. In formal models, MoM is the mapping or fluctuation that produces the first pair of distinguishable states from MoP.

**Meta-Axioms:**
- MoP: Potential for distinction is a primitive property of the null state. Falsifiable if a system can be shown to emerge from a state with no potential for distinction.
- MoM: The first distinction is a necessary event. Falsifiable if emergence is observed without any act of distinction.
- Latent Asymmetry: The null state contains latent asymmetry, realized upon interaction. Falsifiable if all observed emergence is symmetric and indistinguishable.

---

## 3. Statement of the Axiom

**Axiom (Duality of Emergence, Version 2.0):**
Emergence of new structure, information, or behavior occurs if and only if at least two distinguishable states interact via a non-trivial relation, generating properties not present in either state alone. No system of a single undifferentiated state can exhibit emergence. The axiom is non-trivial because it predicts the impossibility of emergence in systems lacking distinction and specifies necessary conditions for emergent phenomena.

**Falsifiability:**
- The axiom is falsified if a system with a single undifferentiated state exhibits emergent properties, or if emergence is observed without relational difference.

## 4. Mathematical Formalization

### 4.1 Set Theory
Let $S$ be a set of states, $|S| \geq 2$. Define a relation $R: S \times S \to P$ where $P$ is the set of possible properties. $a, b \in S$ are distinguishable if $\exists Q$ such that $Q(a) \neq Q(b)$. A property $p \in P$ is emergent if $p \notin \bigcup_{x \in S} Q(x)$ but $p \in R(a, b)$ for some $a \neq b$.

### 4.2 Category Theory
Let $\mathcal{C}$ be a category with objects $A, B$ and morphisms $f: A \to B$. Emergence requires at least two objects and a non-identity morphism $f \neq \mathrm{id}_A$ such that $f \circ g$ yields new structure not present in $A$ or $B$ alone. All terms follow standard category theory (Mac Lane, 1971).

### 4.3 Information Theory
Let $X, Y$ be random variables. Emergence requires $I(X; Y) > 0$ (mutual information), and synergy as defined by Partial Information Decomposition (PID): $H(X, Y) > H(X) + H(Y) - I(X; Y)$. All terms as in Shannon (1948) and Williams & Beer (2010).

### 4.4 Dynamical Systems
Let $S$ be a state space, $F: S \times S \to S$ a generative rule. Emergence occurs if $F(a, b) \notin \{a, b\}$ and $F(a, b)$ exhibits new properties. All terms as in standard dynamical systems theory.

## 5. The First Distinction Problem

- **MoP:** The null state has potential for distinction. Falsifiable if emergence is observed from a state with no such potential.
- **MoM:** The first distinction is a necessary event. Falsifiable if emergence occurs without any act of distinction.
- **Latent Asymmetry:** The null state contains latent asymmetry, realized upon interaction. Falsifiable if all emergence is symmetric and indistinguishable.

---

## 6. Necessary and Sufficient Conditions

- **Necessary:** $|S| \geq 2$, $R(a, b)$ defined for $a \neq b$, recursion possible.
- **Sufficient:** $|S| \geq 2$ and $R(a, b)$ generates a property not present in $a$ or $b$ alone.
- **Impossibility:** $|S| < 2$ or all elements indistinguishable; $R(a, b) = R(a, a)$ for all $a, b$.

---

## 7. Testable Predictions

- No system with a single undifferentiated state can exhibit emergent properties (testable in cellular automata, information theory, and cosmological models).
- All observed emergence is traceable to a minimal duality or distinction (testable in simulation and experiment).
- In cosmology, the earliest observable structure must reflect a primordial distinction (testable via CMB or symmetry-breaking signatures).
- In information theory, synergistic information only appears when variables are distinguishable and related (testable via PID analysis).

---

## 8. Mapping to Scientific Frameworks

- **Symmetry Breaking:** The axiom generalizes spontaneous symmetry breaking as the first act of distinction.
- **Entropy Gradients:** Emergence of force (entropic gravity) requires a difference (duality) in entropy.
- **Holographic Duality:** Boundary/bulk duality is a concrete realization.
- **Generative Recursion:** All recursive growth starts from a minimal distinction.
- **Structuralism:** Relations, not isolated entities, generate meaning and structure.

---

## 9. Limitations and Open Questions

- The “first distinction” remains a meta-axiomatic assumption.
- The axiom does not specify the origin of the potential for distinction (MoP).
- Extension to infinite or continuous state spaces is non-trivial and may require measure theory.
- The role of observation/measurement in quantum systems may require further clarification.
- Empirical challenges: distinguishing true emergence from apparent complexity.
