# Emergence Axiom in Meta-Narrative Math (Revised 2026)

## Statement
The emergence axiom states that new properties, patterns, or complexities arise only when duality (distinction) is present and recursive generativity is possible. Emergence is not the result of isolated states, but of ongoing interaction and synthesis between distinguishable elements, beginning from pure potential (MoP) and the first distinction (MoM).

## Formal Rule
Let $S$ be a system with recursive generative rules, and let $D$ indicate the presence of duality. Emergence occurs when:

$$
D > 0 \ \wedge \ \exists P: P \notin S_0,\ P \in S_n\ \text{for some } n > 0
$$

where $P$ is a property or pattern not present in the initial state $S_0$, but appears in a later state $S_n$ due to recursive application of generative rules, enabled by duality.

## Example
If $S_0$ is pure potential (MoP, $\emptyset$), and after the first distinction ($S_1 = \{E, G\}$), recursive generativity produces new emergent properties (e.g., communities, hierarchies, new forms of organization), then emergence has occurred.

## Interpretation
- Emergence requires duality/distinction and recursive generativity.
- Novelty and complexity arise from the ongoing synthesis and interaction of distinguishable states, not from isolation.
- This axiom unifies narrative, physical, and quantum emergence under the logic of duality and recursion.
