# Stability Axiom in Meta-Narrative Math (Revised 2026)

## Statement
The stability axiom states that the persistence and flourishing of any universe, story, or system require not only generativity, agency, and observation, but also the ongoing presence of duality (distinction) and recursive emergence. Stability is achieved when the system maintains sufficient relational difference and generative interaction to avoid collapse into stasis or chaos.

## Formal Rule
Let $D$ (duality/distinction), $G$ (generativity), $A$ (agency), $O$ (observation), and $T$ (threshold) be defined for a system $S$.

$$
S \text{ is stable} \iff D + G + A + O \geq T
$$

Where:
- $D$ is a measure of ongoing distinction or duality in the system
- $G$ is generativity (capacity for new emergence)
- $A$ is agency (capacity for action)
- $O$ is observation (capacity for awareness/measurement)
- $T$ is the minimum threshold for stability

## Example
If $D = 1$, $G = 1$, $A = 1$, $O = 1$, $T = 3.5$, then $S$ is stable because $1 + 1 + 1 + 1 = 4 \geq 3.5$.

## Interpretation
- Stability requires the ongoing presence of duality/distinction and recursive generativity, not just static properties.
- If duality or generativity drops to zero, the system risks collapse (loss of structure, stasis, or chaos).
- This axiom unifies narrative, physical, and quantum stability under the logic of duality and emergence.
