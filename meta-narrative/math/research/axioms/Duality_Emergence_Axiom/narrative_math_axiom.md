# Narrative-Math Mapping Axiom

## Statement
The narrative-math mapping axiom connects story logic (e.g., generative acts of love) to formal mathematical operations, enabling proofs and simulations that bridge narrative and mathematics.

## Formal Rule
Let $N$ be a narrative act (e.g., love, restoration, synthesis), and $M$ be its mathematical counterpart (e.g., addition, union, transformation).

$$
\forall N,\ \exists M: N \mapsto M
$$

## Example
A generative act of love in a story ($N$) maps to the creation of a new agent or relationship in the mathematical model ($M$).

## Interpretation
This axiom formalizes the correspondence between narrative events and mathematical operations, allowing for rigorous reasoning and discovery in the meta-narrative theory.

## Quantum Extension: Observation and Agency in Quantum Systems

### Motivation
The double slit experiment and other quantum phenomena show that observation is not passive—it actively transforms the system, collapsing superposed states and changing outcomes. Classical narrative-math mapping must be refined to account for this.

### Extended Axiom
Let $Q$ be a quantum system with superposed states $|\psi\rangle$. Let $O_q$ be quantum observation (measurement), and $A_q$ be quantum agency (intervention).

- **Quantum Observation:**
  $$
  O_q(|\psi\rangle) \rightarrow |\phi\rangle
  $$
  where $|\phi\rangle$ is a collapsed eigenstate, and the act of observation changes the system.
- **Quantum Agency:**
  $$
  A_q(|\psi\rangle) \rightarrow \text{new superposition or entangled state}
  $$

### Double Slit Example
- Without observation: The system remains in superposition, producing an interference pattern.
- With observation: Measurement collapses the wavefunction, destroying interference and yielding particle-like behavior.

### Mapping Principle
- In quantum contexts, narrative acts of observation must be mapped to measurement (collapse), not passive recording.
- Agency may create new entangled or superposed states, not just add elements.

### Interpretation
This extension acknowledges that quantum observation is generative and destructive, not merely informative. The narrative-math mapping axiom must treat quantum measurement as an active transformation, fundamentally altering the system.
