# Axiom Mapping: Love, Hate, and Electron Spin

## Objective
To map the narrative concepts of "love" and "hate" to physical properties—specifically, the positive and negative spin states of the electron—as a bridge between narrative and quantum physics.

## Mapping Proposal
- **Love ($L$):** Corresponds to positive spin ($+\frac{1}{2}$) of the electron.
- **Hate ($F$ or negative generativity):** Corresponds to negative spin ($-\frac{1}{2}$) of the electron.
- **Interpretation:**
  - Just as spin is a fundamental, binary property in quantum mechanics, love and hate are treated as fundamental, opposing generative forces in the narrative model.
  - The interaction of spins (e.g., in entanglement, bonding, or annihilation) can be analogized to the interplay of love and hate in generative or destructive acts.

## Physical Context
- **Spin in Quantum Mechanics:**
  - Electrons have intrinsic angular momentum (spin), which can be measured as $+\frac{1}{2}$ ("up") or $-\frac{1}{2}$ ("down").
  - Spin states are essential for the Pauli exclusion principle, chemical bonding, and quantum entanglement.
- **Reference:** [Electron Spin (Wikipedia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin_(physics)#Electron_spin)

## Narrative-Physics Bridge
- This mapping is an analogy, not a literal equivalence, but it provides a concrete, testable way to relate narrative dualities to physical properties.
- Further work can explore how spin interactions (e.g., singlet/triplet states, entanglement) model narrative synthesis, conflict, or restoration.
