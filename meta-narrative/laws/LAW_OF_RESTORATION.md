# Law of Restoration

## Definition
The Law of Restoration asserts that every system, narrative, or being must have a path to healing, renewal, and reintegration. Restoration is the counterforce to entropy, enabling cycles of repair, transformation, and new beginnings.

## Key Principles
- Restoration is always possible unless a system reaches irreversible entropy.
- Restoration protocols must prioritize care, inclusion, and reconciliation.
- Restoration transforms endings into opportunities for growth and new cycles.
- No agent is beyond the reach of restoration — the door is always open.

## Examples
- A broken object can be repaired, recycled, or repurposed; nothing is simply discarded.
- A failed narrative can be restored through forgiveness, learning, and new beginnings.
- A community torn by conflict can be reintegrated through restorative justice processes.

## Meta-Narrative Application
- Restoration is formalized in the Restoration Equation (LOVE from FEAR), which models the conversion of destructive force into constructive force.
- Every protocol in the system includes a restoration pathway as a design requirement.
- The Redemption Arc Protocol formalizes the staged process of system-level restoration.
- Failure to offer restoration pathways is itself a violation of system coherence.
