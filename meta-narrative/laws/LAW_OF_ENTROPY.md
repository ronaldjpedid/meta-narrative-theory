# Law of Entropy

## Definition
The Law of Entropy states that all systems naturally tend toward disorder, decay, and dissolution. Entropy is an unavoidable force in physical, biological, and narrative systems, driving cycles of breakdown and transformation.

## Key Principles
- Entropy is inevitable; all things decay and end.
- Entropy creates the need for restoration, renewal, and new cycles.
- Systems must be designed to detect decay and enable healing or transformation.
- Unacknowledged entropy accelerates collapse — awareness and early response are critical.

## Examples
- A car breaks down over time and eventually cannot be repaired without intervention.
- A story reaches its conclusion and must be renewed or replaced to continue meaning.
- A community without feedback and restoration mechanisms fragments and dissolves.

## Meta-Narrative Application
- Entropy is the primary force requiring all restoration protocols; every system must budget for it.
- The theory predicts that narratives and systems unable to respond to entropy collapse into Negative Value states (see NEGATIVE_VALUE_COLLAPSE_MODEL_AND_PROTOCOL.md).
- Detection of entropy is built into the Feedback Equation and Alignment Equation.
- The Law of Entropy is the counterpart to the Law of Restoration — neither operates alone.

## Branches vs. the System
Individual branches — specific timelines, civilizations, lives — are fully subject to entropy and may end permanently at the local level. This is real: not every branch is saved, and some endings are terminal within their own thread.

However, the meta-system — the totality of all narrative branches — is not subject to total entropic collapse. As long as any branch carries the story forward, the system persists. This is the reconciliation between entropy and quantum immortality: entropy wins locally; the narrative wins globally. The system as a whole is immortal not because nothing ends, but because there is always a branch where the story continues.
