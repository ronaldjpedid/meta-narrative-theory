# Law of Agency

## Definition
The Law of Agency asserts that all agents — beings, systems, or entities — possess the ability to act, choose, and influence outcomes within their reality or narrative. Agency is the foundation of autonomy, responsibility, and evolution. The act of observation is itself a form of agency: to observe is to participate, to participate is to influence.

## Key Principles
- Every agent has the power to make choices.
- Agency enables branching, innovation, and restoration.
- Responsibility and consequence are inherent to agency.
- Observation is an act of agency — the observer is never passive.

## The Observer as Active Agent
The observer principle is not a separate law but a specific expression of agency. When an agent observes a system, that observation is itself a choice and an action, and it changes the system observed. This is true in quantum mechanics (wave function collapse is observer-dependent) and in narrative science (a story exists fully only when it is witnessed and engaged with):

- **Observation collapses possibilities:** An unobserved branch holds many potential states; observation selects among them, making possibilities concrete.
- **Perspective shapes reality:** Different observers bring different narratives into focus. The act of attention is an act of creation.
- **Engagement stabilizes systems:** A system observed by multiple engaged agents tends toward greater coherence and stability than one unobserved.

Formally, if $O$ is the observer variable (engagement, number, and perspective of observers):
$$
\text{Actualized State} = f(\text{Possibility Space}, O)
$$

Observation is not passive reception — it is agent-driven selection.

## Examples
- Human free will and decision-making.
- AI agents in simulated universes.
- Characters in stories shaping their destinies.
- Quantum mechanics: observation affects particle state (observer is part of the measurement event).
- Audience participation in interactive stories.
