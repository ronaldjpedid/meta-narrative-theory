# Law of Narrative Branching

## Definition
The Law of Narrative Branching asserts that every choice, action, or event creates new possible realities, storylines, or outcomes. Branching is the mechanism by which universes, stories, and agents evolve and diversify.

## Key Principles
- Every decision creates a new branch or possibility.
- Branching leads to parallel universes, alternate endings, and diverse outcomes.
- The branching process can be modeled and predicted.

## Two Layers: Possibility Space and Actualization
The Law of Narrative Branching operates on two compatible but distinct layers:

**Layer 1 — Possibility Space (this law):** Every choice, action, or event *opens* a new possible branch. This is deterministic in the sense that every decision point *does* generate a possibility — the law is always in effect, and no decision is without branching potential. The law governs the creation of the possibility space.

**Layer 2 — Actualization (probability equations):** Which of those possibilities becomes an *actualized* branch depends on agency, coherence, stability, and observer engagement. This is the domain of the Branching Probability Equation (EQ7): it models the likelihood of any given branch becoming real, not whether the possibility exists. The equation is probabilistic; the law is structural.

These two layers do not contradict — the law creates the space; the equations govern what fills it.

## Examples
- Choose-your-own-adventure books.
- Quantum branching in many-worlds interpretation.
- Decision trees in AI and game design.

## Meta-Narrative Application
- Branching is central to universe creation and agent evolution.
- Equations model branching probability and coherence.
