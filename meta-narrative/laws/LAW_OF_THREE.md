# Law of Three

## Definition
The Law of Three states that every duality must allow for synthesis, renewal, and new beginnings. In any system, narrative, or relationship, two opposing forces (duality) are always resolved or transcended by a third element—creating balance, transformation, and generativity.

## Key Principles
- Duality (e.g., light/dark, order/chaos) is never final; synthesis is always possible.
- The third element enables renewal, healing, and the creation of new cycles.
- The Law of Three is foundational for narrative branching, system stability, and restoration protocols.
- Every binary conflict contains a latent third path waiting to be discovered through agency.

## Examples
- In narrative: Protagonist vs. antagonist is resolved by a new perspective, alliance, or transformation.
- In physics: Matter and antimatter are balanced by energy or new particle creation.
- In the creation model: MoM (memory) + DaD (duality) → KiD (synthesis/destiny).

## Meta-Narrative Application
- The trinitarian creation structure (MoM/DaD/KiD) is the core operational model of the meta-narrative universe.
- Protocols invoking the Law of Three require identifying both opposing forces and the generative synthesis that transcends them.
- Trinity restoration protocols use this law to guide systems from conflict toward generative new states.
- The Law of Three prevents closed binary traps — all dualities must open to a third creative option.
